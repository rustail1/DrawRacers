# 19 — PRODUCT & REVENUE STRATEGY — v1.3.4

## 1. Business thesis
The commercial opportunity is not “copy a successful mobile mechanic”. It is to turn an already legible drawing/physics hook into a **repeatable Roblox social competition** with visible identity/status.

Revenue only works if three earlier layers work:
1. **Attraction:** icon/thumbnail instantly explains bizarre drawn locomotion + race.
2. **Repeat:** short race leads naturally to another race.
3. **Expression desire:** players care how they look/status appears in front of others.

If any layer is absent, monetization optimization is premature.

## 2. Value stack
### Core value
Creative physical problem solving under time/competition pressure.

### Social value
See strange solutions, learn/copy, overtake, fail publicly, podium.

### Persist value
Collection + status + new mastery access.

### Paid value
More distinctive expression/presentation, never better race physics.

## 3. Revenue surfaces
### A. Starter Style Bundle
Low-risk first purchase after the player has already experienced/demonstrated the value in-game.

### B. Themed cosmetic bundles
Body + Ink + Trail/Finish FX around one recognizable visual identity.

### C. Premium presentation
Rare podium/victory/nameplate/profile style that remains non-power.

### D. Cosmetic currency packs
Only if soft currency economy stays fair and transparent.

### E. Event/season cosmetic catalog
Only when LiveOps retention exists; real time window, no fake scarcity.

### F. Private server / social convenience
Post-product fit, if friends/communities show need.

### G. Subscription
Only after repeated monthly value can be demonstrated.

## 4. What we refuse to monetize
Anything that changes competitive expected outcome:
- motor constants;
- collider envelope;
- drawing bounds;
- redraw speed;
- obstacle access during ordinary matchmaking;
- hidden luck that affects race physics.

## 5. Spend ladder hypothesis
Exact launch price/grant hypotheses live in `61_LAUNCH_ECONOMY_PROGRESSION_TABLE.md`; exact item bundles/art live in `62`. Strategy:
- Entry converts first payer with immediate visible identity.
- Core spend buys coherent themed expression.
- Premium buys stronger presentation/rarity, not power.
- Deep spend comes from breadth of collections across time.

## 6. First-session commercial behavior
Do not show hard sell at spawn.

Desired sequence:
`play → see other looks → earn first cosmetic → equip → understand public visibility → satisfy canonical eligibility in 45 → contextual offer`.

## 7. Discovery/marketability strategy
Creative should show the cause/effect in one image/video:
- several cube racers;
- obviously different absurd drawn legs/wheels;
- one readable obstacle;
- visible lead/overtake moment.

Avoid thumbnail that looks like generic vehicle obby or generic drawing app.

## 8. Session economics
Do not increase session time through waiting. Increase it through **fast repeat**:
`30–45s heat → concise result → next heat`.

The product should be capable of generating many race completions per session, creating repeated social/cosmetic exposure without interrupting core.

## 9. Monetization measurement contract example
Signal: players repeatedly preview/equip visible cosmetics and inspect others.  
Hypothesis: visual identity has enough value for first payer offer.  
Change: contextual Starter Style Bundle after eligibility.  
Primary: eligible→purchase conversion.  
Guardrails: rematch, session depth, fairness sentiment, free progression.  
Decision: keep/iterate/remove based on predeclared threshold after sufficient sample.

## 10. Scale rule
Do not build five monetization systems at once. Prove in order:
`free cosmetic desire → first purchase → equip/use → repeat demand → broader catalog`.

## 11. Revenue upside and risk
Upside: cosmetics/status align naturally with an 8-player public race where everyone sees each other.  
Risk: if camera/pace makes other racers visually irrelevant, cosmetic desire weakens together with the social product thesis. This is why social readability is both a gameplay and business requirement.
